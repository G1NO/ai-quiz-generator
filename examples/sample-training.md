# Using AI Assistants at Work

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Claude can save time on everyday work, if you use them well. This guide covers four habits: Goal, Context, Format and Check.

## What AI is good at, and what it's not

AI assistants are strong with words. Good uses include drafting emails and documents, summarizing long text or your own notes, changing the tone of something you wrote, brainstorming ideas, and explaining a topic in simpler language.

Be careful with exact facts and figures, math, recent events and prices, and decisions about people. AI can state wrong information confidently. It also doesn't know anything about your company unless you tell it.

## Writing a better prompt

The AI only knows what you tell it. A strong prompt has three parts:

- **Goal:** what you want, stated specifically. "Write a short apology email to a customer" is better than "write an email."
- **Context:** what the AI needs to know. Who is it for? What happened? What are you offering?
- **Format:** what the answer should look like: length, tone and structure.

Example of a weak prompt: "Write an email about the order."
Example of a strong prompt: "Write a short apology email to a long-time client about a one-week delay caused by our supplier. Offer free shipping on their next order. Warm but professional, under 120 words."

## Protecting company data

Whatever you paste into an AI tool may be stored, and depending on the tool, it may be used for training. Never paste customer personal information (names with account numbers, addresses, ID numbers), passwords, confidential financials, unreleased plans, or HR and medical information about coworkers.

Before you paste, ask: would I be OK if this showed up outside the company? If not, replace real details with placeholders like [Customer] or [Account #] and fill them in yourself afterward. Always follow your company's AI policy.

## Checking the output

AI sometimes makes things up: names, numbers, dates, policies and even links. This is called a hallucination. Before you use anything an AI writes, run a 3-step check:

1. **Facts:** verify names, numbers, dates and policies against a real source.
2. **Fit:** does it say what you mean, in the right tone?
3. **Final read:** read it as the person who will receive it.

Asking the AI "are you sure?" is not a reliable check. It may repeat a wrong answer confidently.
