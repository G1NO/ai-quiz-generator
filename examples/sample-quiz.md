# Using AI Assistants at Work

### 1. Which of these tasks is the best fit for an AI assistant?
*Objective: Learners will be able to identify tasks that are a good fit for an AI assistant. · Bloom's level: understand*

- **A.** Drafting a first version of a team announcement ✅
- **B.** Calculating final payroll totals for the month
- **C.** Deciding which employee gets a promotion
- **D.** Confirming a vendor's price for today

<details><summary>Feedback</summary>

- **A:** Drafting is a strength. You review and edit before sending.
- **B:** AI can make math errors. Use your payroll system or a spreadsheet.
- **C:** Decisions about people need human judgment and context the AI doesn't have.
- **D:** The AI may give an outdated or invented number. Check the vendor directly.

</details>

### 2. Jordan needs to tell a long-time client their order is delayed one week by a supplier. Which prompt will get the most usable first draft?
*Objective: Learners will be able to write a prompt that includes a goal, context and format. · Bloom's level: apply*

- **A.** "Write an email to our client about their order being delayed by our supplier."
- **B.** "Write a short apology email to a long-time client about a one-week supplier delay. Warm, under 120 words." ✅
- **C.** "Write a professional email that is warm, friendly and under 120 words, with a helpful tone."
- **D.** "Our supplier delayed a long-time client's order by one week, and they are a valued customer."

<details><summary>Feedback</summary>

- **A:** It has a goal and some context, but no format, so the AI has to guess the tone and length.
- **B:** It has a clear goal, the context (who, what, why) and the format (tone and length).
- **C:** Format only. The AI doesn't know what happened or who the email is for.
- **D:** Context only. There's no goal, so the AI doesn't know what you want it to write.

</details>

### 3. A coworker wants to paste a customer's name, address and account number into a public AI tool to draft a letter. What should they do instead?
*Objective: Learners will be able to decide what information is safe to share with an AI tool. · Bloom's level: apply*

- **A.** Replace the details with placeholders like [Customer] and fill them in afterward ✅
- **B.** Paste it in, since most AI tools delete chat history automatically after a while
- **C.** Paste it in, as long as the customer has been with the company for years
- **D.** Share just the account number and leave the customer's name and address out

<details><summary>Feedback</summary>

- **A:** The AI drafts the letter, and the personal data never leaves your hands.
- **B:** Many tools store what you paste. Don't assume it gets deleted.
- **C:** How long someone has been a customer doesn't change the data risk.
- **D:** An account number on its own is still sensitive. Use a placeholder instead.

</details>

### 4. The AI gives you a statistic to put in a report for leadership. What should you do first?
*Objective: Learners will be able to review AI output for errors before using it. · Bloom's level: apply*

- **A.** Confirm it in a reliable source before you use it ✅
- **B.** Ask the AI to double-check whether the number is right
- **C.** Round the number so it looks cleaner in the report
- **D.** Use it as-is, since AI tools are usually accurate

<details><summary>Feedback</summary>

- **A:** AI can invent numbers that sound real. Facts always get checked.
- **B:** The AI may repeat the same wrong answer confidently. Check a real source.
- **C:** Rounding a made-up number doesn't make it true.
- **D:** "Usually" isn't good enough for a report with your name on it.

</details>

### 5. Which of the following is NOT one of the four prompt habits?
*Objective: not set · Bloom's level: remember*

- **A.** Goal
- **B.** Context
- **C.** Speed, because a fast prompt always gets the best answer from the assistant ✅
- **D.** All of the above

<details><summary>Feedback</summary>

- **A:** Incorrect.
- **B:** Incorrect.
- **C:** Correct.
- **D:** Incorrect.

</details>

> ⚠️ **Review:** Uses "all/none of the above". These let learners guess from partial knowledge. Negative stem (NOT/EXCEPT/LEAST). Learners often misread these; rephrase if you can. Correct answer is much longer than the others. Length can give the answer away. 4 option(s) have very short feedback. Explain *why* the choice is right or wrong. No learning objective attached.
